# Post Formats

Nine shapes. Pick by what the raw thought actually is, not by what you posted last time.
Each shows structure, then a worked skeleton. Character counts are the target zone,
not a rule.

---

## 1. The Autopsy — something failed

*Use when:* you shipped it, it didn't work, and you understand why now.
*Length:* 1,100–1,600. *Highest-trust format on the platform.*

```
[Hook: the loss, stated flatly. A number if you have one.]

[What you expected to happen.]

[What actually happened — one concrete detail that makes it real.]

[The thing you missed, and why it was easy to miss.]

[What you do instead now.]

[Close: the one line someone should take away. No moral, no "lesson learned".]
```

## 2. The Number — one surprising metric

*Use when:* you have a real figure that contradicts an assumption.
*Length:* 700–1,100.

```
[Hook: the number, bare. "34% of our checks never finish."]

[Why you assumed otherwise.]

[How you found out.]

[What the number actually meant once you dug in — usually not the obvious read.]

[What changed as a result.]
```

Never open with a number you can't source from your own system.

## 3. The Reversal — you changed your mind

*Use when:* you held a position, tested it, and now hold the opposite.
*Length:* 900–1,400.

```
[Hook: the position you used to hold, stated without hedging.]

[Why it seemed right. Be fair to your past self — a straw man kills the post.]

[The thing that broke it.]

[Where you landed.]

[The part you're still not sure about.]  ← this line is why people trust the post
```

## 4. The Teardown — a specific artifact

*Use when:* you can point at one screen, flow, or product surface.
*Length:* 800–1,300. Pairs with an image or carousel.

```
[Hook: the specific thing + the specific verdict.]

[Observation 1 — what they did, why it works or doesn't.]
[Observation 2]
[Observation 3]

[The one you'd steal.]
```

Be generous to the maker. Teardowns that dunk age badly and get quoted back at you.

## 5. The Build Log — shipped this week

*Use when:* Friday, and you shipped something.
*Length:* 600–1,000.

```
[Hook: the thing, in plain language, no launch-speak.]

[Why it existed — the user problem in one sentence.]

[The part that was harder than expected.]

[What it does now.]

[What's next, or an honest "no idea yet".]
```

## 6. The Question You Got Asked

*Use when:* a real person asked you something real.
*Length:* 700–1,200.

```
[Hook: the question, in their words, in quotes.]

[Why it's the right question / why it's slightly the wrong question.]

[Your actual answer, with the caveat.]

[What you'd need to know to give a better one.]
```

## 7. The Constraint — what you refuse to do

*Use when:* you have a strong operating principle and pay a real cost for it.
*Length:* 700–1,100.

```
[Hook: the refusal. "ContentIntel will never store your API key on our servers."]

[What it costs you — be specific, this is the whole credibility of the post.]

[Why you pay it anyway.]

[What you'd need to see to change your mind.]
```

## 8. The Carousel — teaching something in steps

*Use when:* the idea has 5–9 discrete steps and benefits from pacing.
*Format:* PDF document post, 1080×1350, 6–10 slides.
*Post body:* 400–700 chars — a hook plus one line of context. The slides carry it.

```
Slide 1  The promise, huge type. 6 words max.
Slide 2  The problem, concretely.
Slide 3–8 One idea per slide. One sentence + one supporting line.
Slide 9  The summary — all points on one slide, so it's screenshot-able.
Slide 10 Who you are, one line. No hard CTA.
```

Readable at thumbnail size or it doesn't exist. See `linkedin-visual` skill.

## 9. The Short One

*Use when:* the thought is complete in 40 words and padding would ruin it.
*Length:* 200–400.

```
[Observation, sharply.]

[The consequence.]
```

No hook/body structure — the whole post is the hook. Use rarely. When it works
it's the best thing on the feed; when it doesn't, it's a tweet on the wrong platform.

## Hook patterns

The hook is the ~140 characters before "…see more". These shapes work because
they open a loop the reader needs closed.

| Pattern | Example shape |
|---|---|
| **Flat loss** | "We deleted the feature that took me three weeks." |
| **Contradiction** | "Our slowest page has the highest conversion." |
| **Bare number** | "One user generated 40% of last month's API cost." |
| **Confession** | "I was wrong about [thing] for about eight months." |
| **Refusal** | "I turned down the integration everyone asked for." |
| **Overheard** | "'This is just ChatGPT with extra steps.' — a user, last Tuesday." |
| **Specific time** | "Two weeks in, I threw away the entire schema." |
| **Direct contradiction of consensus** | "Shipping fast made our product worse." |

**Hook failure modes:** starting with context, starting with "I've been thinking
about", asking a question the reader doesn't care about, promising a list
("5 things I learned"), and any sentence that could open any post on the platform.

---

## CTA

Most posts need none. The post ending well *is* the call to action.

When you do want one:
- A real question you'll actually answer in the comments.
- "If you've solved this, I want to hear how" — only if true.
- Never: "Follow for more", "Agree?", "DM me", "Comment X below".
