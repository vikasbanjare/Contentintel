# Engagement Rules

Two jobs: commenting on other people's posts, and replying in your own comment section.
The first is how strangers discover you. The second is what decides whether they stay.

Budget: **~20 minutes a day, 8–12 comments.** More than that and quality drops,
which defeats the point.

---

## Part 1 — Commenting on other people's posts

### REPLY when at least two are true

- The post is **under ~90 minutes old** — early comments get seen by everyone who
  arrives after you.
- You have **first-hand experience** with the exact thing being discussed.
- You can **disagree productively** or add a detail the post is missing.
- The author is someone you'd want a conversation with in a year.
- The comment section is still **small enough that you'd be read** (under ~40 comments).
- The post is in one of your pillars, so the audience you attract is the right one.

### SKIP — always

- Anything political, religious, or about an ongoing conflict.
- Grief, illness, death, layoffs. If you genuinely know them, send a DM instead.
- Obvious engagement bait ("Comment 'GUIDE' and I'll send it"). Commenting rewards it.
- Rage-bait and contrarian-for-clicks posts. You cannot win that thread.
- Threads already at 200+ comments — you're invisible.
- Anything where your honest comment would be an advert for ContentIntel.
- A post you don't actually have an opinion about. "Great post!" is worse than silence —
  it's a public record that you comment without reading.
- Someone attacking a third party. Don't pile on.

### SKIP — usually, use judgement

- Posts by people with a huge following where the comment section is a slot machine.
- Personal-milestone posts (new job, anniversary) — a reaction is enough, a comment
  from a stranger is odd.
- Threads where you'd be the fourth person making the same point.

### What a good comment is

**Length:** 2–4 sentences. Long enough to be a contribution, short enough to read.
No essays — if you have an essay, that's your own post.

**Shape, pick one:**
- *The specific counter* — "We tried this at ~2k users and it broke in a way I didn't
  expect: [one detail]. Ended up doing [alternative] instead."
- *The missing variable* — "This holds if [condition]. The case where it doesn't is
  [case], which is where most people I know got stuck."
- *The receipt* — agree, but add the number or artifact that makes it concrete.
- *The honest question* — a real question you'd want the answer to, not a fake one
  to seem engaged. Only if you'd actually read a long reply.

**Never:**
- Generic praise. "Great insight", "So true", "Well said", "This 💯"
- Emoji-only
- Your link, your product, your newsletter. Not once, not subtly.
- "Following for more" / "Commenting for reach"
- Restating the post back at the author
- Correcting a trivial detail to look sharp
- Anything you wouldn't say to their face at a conference

**Tone:** you're a peer adding something, not a fan and not an examiner.

### After you comment

- If they reply, reply back once — within the hour if you can. That second exchange
  is where connections actually form.
- Don't comment twice on the same post.
- Don't follow up with a connection request the same day. It reads as transactional.

## Part 2 — Your own comment section

This is the higher-leverage half. A post with 30 comments where you replied
substantively to all 30 outperforms one with 60 you ignored.

### Priority order, first 90 minutes

1. **Substantive comments and disagreement** — reply first, reply longest.
2. **Real questions** — answer properly, and the answer often becomes next week's post.
3. **People you want to know** — reply with something that opens a second exchange.
4. **Short praise** — a warm one-liner back, with a question attached if there's
   anything genuine to ask. Never leave these unanswered, but don't spend the
   time you owe group 1 on them.
5. **Tagged-a-friend comments** — thank them, address the friend by name if they show up.

### Reply craft

- **Extend, don't close.** A reply ending in a full stop kills the thread. A reply
  ending in a genuine question keeps it alive — but only if the question is real.
- **Use their name.** Once, at the start or not at all.
- **Match length roughly.** A three-word comment doesn't get a paragraph back.
- **Longer than 8–10 words.** Very short replies read as dismissive and are
  reportedly weighted less.
- **Never copy-paste the same reply.** It's visible in the thread and it's the
  single clearest signal that a bot is running the account.

### Handling disagreement

- **Good-faith disagreement:** the best thing that can happen to a post. Concede the
  part they're right about explicitly, then hold the part you're right about. Don't
  win — clarify.
- **Bad-faith or hostile:** one calm, short, factual reply. Then stop replying. Never
  a second one.
- **Personal attack or abuse:** no reply. Delete the comment, report if it warrants it.
  Deleting is not losing.
- **Someone says it's AI-written:** if it isn't, say so plainly once and move on.
  Don't get defensive, don't over-explain.

### Corrections

If you got a fact wrong and someone catches it, correct it in a reply immediately and
name them. Don't quietly edit the post and pretend. This buys more trust than the
original post was ever going to.

---

## Weekly targets

| | Target |
|---|---|
| Posts | 3–5 |
| Comments on others' posts | 40–60 |
| Reply rate on own comments | ~100% within 24h |
| New conversations that moved to DM | 2–3 |

Track the last row, not follower count. Followers are a vanity number; conversations
are the thing that actually turns into users, jobs, or collaborators.
