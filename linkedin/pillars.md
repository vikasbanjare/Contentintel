# Content Pillars

Four lanes. Every post belongs to exactly one. If a thought doesn't fit a lane,
either it's a new lane or it's not a post.

Rough weekly mix: **Building 40% · AI in practice 30% · Design 20% · Industry read 10%**

The mix matters more than any single post. All-building reads as a changelog.
All-opinion reads as a pundit with nothing shipped.

---

## 1. Building in public — ContentIntel

What you actually shipped, broke, or killed this week.

Strongest angles:
- A feature you built and then deleted, and why
- A number that surprised you (usage, cost, latency, churn)
- A user said one sentence that changed the roadmap
- The unglamorous part nobody writes about (billing, auth, rate limits)
- A decision you'd make differently now

Weakest angles (avoid): "excited to launch", feature announcements with no story,
roadmap screenshots.

## 2. AI in practice

What breaks when you actually put a model in production, as opposed to in a demo.

Strongest angles:
- Prompt/architecture change that moved a real metric
- Where the model is reliably bad and what you do instead
- Cost or latency reality vs. what the demo implied
- A thing everyone recommends that didn't work for you
- Evals: how you actually know it got better

Weakest angles: model release commentary with no hands-on, "AI will change everything",
tool listicles.

## 3. Design & craft

Interface, visual, and product-craft opinions — backed by a specific artifact.

Strongest angles:
- Before/after of your own screen, with the reasoning
- A UI pattern that tests badly and keeps getting copied
- Why a "worse-looking" version converted better
- Constraint that forced a better design
- Teardown of a product you admire — specific, not fawning

Weakest angles: generic design principles, Figma tips with no context, "good design is…"

## 4. Industry read

Rare. Only when you have an angle nobody else has, usually because you're
operating in the space.

Strongest angles:
- Something the mainstream take gets backwards, with your evidence
- A shift you can see from your own data
- Prediction you're willing to be graded on later

Weakest angles: news reaction, hot take on a funding round, doom or hype.

## Angle bank

When a raw thought is thin, run it through these. Each one is a different post.

1. **The reversal** — "Everyone says X. I did X for six months. Here's what happened."
2. **The number** — one surprising metric, then the story behind it.
3. **The autopsy** — a thing that failed, dissected without self-flagellation.
4. **The receipt** — a claim plus the artifact that proves it (screenshot, diff, chart).
5. **The before/after** — same problem, two solutions, what changed between them.
6. **The unpopular constraint** — "I refuse to do X" and the cost you accept for it.
7. **The question you got asked** — a real DM or user question, answered properly.
8. **The thing you were wrong about** — highest-trust format on the platform.
9. **The tiny detail** — a 2% change that mattered. Specificity is the whole post.
10. **The comparison** — two approaches, honest trade-offs, your pick and why.

## Series worth running

Named, recurring formats compound — people start recognising them.

- **"Shipped this week"** — Friday, one thing, with the messy part included.
- **"I was wrong about…"** — monthly, whatever you actually changed your mind on.
- **"Teardown"** — one product surface, three specific observations.

---

## Do not post about

- Politics, religion, ongoing conflicts
- Layoffs or grief as a hook for your own point
- Anything under NDA, any client name without written permission
- Other people's revenue, usage, or private conversations
- Anything you'd be uncomfortable defending in a reply from the person it's about
